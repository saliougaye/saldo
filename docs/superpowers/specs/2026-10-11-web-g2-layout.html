The G2 layout of the web app

On a wide screen, a sidebar replaces the top bar. A detail panel is a dialog over the page. Home and the other pages follow the approved mockups.

Language Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100) Source Spec §17.1.7 · decisions D1–D13 Plan 2026-10-11-web-g2-redesign.md Date 2026-10-11
01

Scope

This spec covers the web layout at and above 1024px. Below 1024px, §17.1.7 of the POC spec stays as it is.

The user approved these decisions on 2026-10-11. Two of them come from the plan, not from the user. They are marked “plan”.

All decisions are for the web only. The phone does not change. D5 is the one that could affect the phone, and it does not: the phone keeps §5.13.

On the topics below, this spec wins over §17.1.7. It also replaces two earlier decisions. Section 07 lists them.

Designs

Home: mockups/2026-10-11-web-home-polish-variants.html?variant=G2. The other pages: mockups/2026-10-11-web-pages-g2-variants.html?variant=K. Where the code and a mockup differ, this spec describes the code.

02

The shell

One sidebar on the leading side. In Profile and Admin, the sub-navigation takes its place.

The sidebar (D1). It is 232px wide (layout.sidebarWidth) and as high as the window. It sticks to the top when the page scrolls. It has three parts, from top to bottom.

  1. The brand: the Rocky tile (D2) and the name.
  2. The destinations in one column: Home, Transactions, Statistics, Accounts, and Admin for the admin role.
  3. At the foot: “New transaction” with the key N, then the profile link with the avatar and the name.

The sidebar is a display: none block below 1024px. There, the top bar and the tab bar navigate.

The section sidebar (D10). Profile and Admin have their own sub-navigation. At and above 1024px, it is fixed over the sidebar, with the same width. The shell hides the sidebar (data-section-nav), so the page does not move when you enter Profile.

The sub-navigation starts with “‹ Saldo”. It goes back to Home. The links follow. “Sign out” and the items after the rule sit at the foot of the column. Below 1024px, the sub-navigation is a row of links.

The sub-navigation enters with @starting-style: it moves 8px from the trailing side and fades in, in 200ms. It plays on each mount of the layout, which means each time you enter Profile or Admin from another section. A move between two Profile pages does not play it. With prefers-reduced-motion, only the fade plays.

03

The panel is a dialog

A detail opens over the page, with a scrim under it. The page does not move or change its layout.

Shape (D3). At and above 1024px, the panel is 420px wide (--layout-panel-width). It has 16px of margin at the top, the bottom and the trailing edge. Its radius is --radius-dialog. The scrim is --color-scrim. Below 1024px, nothing changes: the panel is the whole screen, or a bottom sheet for a form.

Markup (D13). The panel is <div role="dialog" aria-modal="true" data-panel> with an aria-label. The scrim is a link with aria-hidden="true" and tabindex="-1". It is not a second control.

Close. Three actions close the panel: the ✕, a click on the scrim, and Esc. The ✕ and the scrim are plain links to the same page without the selection. Thus a reload keeps the panel open, and a modified click opens the list in a new tab.

The keyboard. Esc does nothing while the focus is in a field: the field uses the key. Opening and closing from the keyboard play no motion. The keys N, E, ↑ and ↓ still work while a detail is open. isEditable and ignoresShortcut do not treat a [data-panel] dialog as a dialog that owns the keys. Its forms own the keys themselves.

The pointer. The scrim covers the page. A click on another row first closes the dialog. On Transactions, ↑ and ↓ move through the rows while the dialog is open.

MomentFocus
The panel opensThe focus goes to the heading of the panel. If a form inside already took the focus (its first field), the form keeps it.
The panel is openTab and Shift+Tab stay inside the dialog. From outside, they go to the first or the last control.
The panel closesThe panel unmounts. The focus goes back to the element that had it before the panel opened, for example the row. If the person moved the focus elsewhere meanwhile, it stays there.
04

Home

A head, then three sections in a column: Transactions, Budget and Goals.

The head (D4). It has two columns, 7 and 5. On the left: the period with the stepper beside the title, “Left to spend” at 56px, then Income and Expenses under it. On the right: “Period trend”.

Transactions. The section has two columns, 7 and 5. “Recent transactions” (the last 7) is on the left. “Upcoming” (5 rows) is on the right.

Budget. The cards are in a grid of 3 columns. Each card shows a ring, the amount that is left, a line “of {limit} · {percent}”, a bar of 8px and the advice per day.

The big figure (D5). The big figure of a budget card is what is left, not what is spent. This is for the web only. The phone keeps §5.13.

Not budgeted (D6). The last card of the section shows the spend of the period in categories that no budget covers. If a budget covers every category with spend, the card shows no amount. unbudgetedSpend in @saldo/core computes the figure. It has tests.

Goals. Goals are cards, in the same grid.

05

The other pages

Transactions, Statistics and Accounts follow the variant “K · Scelta”. Each page takes the best part of each variant.

Transactions (D7, D12). Three cards open the page. “Expenses” has the pace bar. “Income” has the total. “Upcoming” has the count, the total and the next three rows. Under the cards: the search, the chips and the days. The “Upcoming” disclosure stays in the list (§5.12), and ↑ ↓ walk through it. The mockup had no disclosure, because it had only two upcoming rows.

Statistics (D8). The head is the head of Home: “Expenses” at 56px, then Income, Net and the projection. “Period trend” is on the right. Cashflow runs across the page. The breakdown follows, as category cards. A category card opens a panel. The panel shows the figure of the category, its last periods and its transactions. Tags come last.

Accounts (D9). The head is the head of Home: “Net worth” at 56px, “Spendable total”, and a sparkline of the net worth on the right. Accounts and goals follow, as cards in a grid of 3 columns. A card opens the panel of the account or of the goal. “New account” is the last card.

Profile and Admin (D10). Section 02 describes them.

06

Polish

The review of G2 (D11) set these values. Each value is a token or comes from a token.

WhereValueWhy
Press: cards, open rows, nav links, “New transaction”scale(0.96) in 120ms (--motion-press)The user presses these many times a day. The answer must feel direct. With prefers-reduced-motion, the scale is 1.
Nav link, hoverraised at 60%Hover and current are two strengths of one surface.
Nav link, currentraised at 100%See above.
Card, hoverThe border stays. A shadow of two layers is added.Shadows give depth. Borders give structure.
Focus ring2px, offset 2px, with the radius of the elementThe ring must follow the shape: 14px on a card, 12px on a button.
Legend under a bar13px, text-muted11px in text-faint did not pass the contrast check.
Panel and scrim, enter200ms, 12px from the trailing edge, cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)The panel arrives from the edge where it will leave.
Panel and scrim, exit150msThe user has decided. The system answers faster.
Panel from the keyboardNo entrance and no exitA keyboard action must not wait for an animation.
Section sidebar@starting-style, 8px, 200ms (--motion-fade), on each mount of the layoutA rare change of place. A spatial signal tells the user that they are inside Profile.
07

What this replaces

Two earlier decisions give way to D3. Do not follow them on the wide screen.

Caution

The spec of 2026-10-07 and the plan of 2026-10-11 (navigation feel) say that the panel sits beside the list and that the web app has no dialogs. Both are old. Follow this spec.

The motion values of the 2026-10-07 spec still apply: the panel opens in the frame of the click, and the keyboard plays no motion.
